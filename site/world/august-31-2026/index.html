FMB Worldwide | August 31, 2026

Three verified developments defined the August 31 global desk: renewed U.S.–Iran attacks and higher oil prices, Nepal’s worsening Bhote Koshi flood disaster, and the sentencing of South Korea’s Unification Church leader in a political-influence corruption case.

Iran · Middle East

U.S.–Iran fire resumes as oil prices jump

Reuters and AP reported renewed U.S.–Iran attacks after a roughly month-long lull. Oil prices rose more than 2% as markets repriced supply risk around the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters

The Philippines imports most of its petroleum requirements, so sustained disruption can feed into pump prices, freight costs and inflation.

Nepal · South Asia

Nepal says 903 dead, about 4,200 missing after Bhote Koshi flood

Nepal’s government reported 903 bodies recovered and about 4,200 people unaccounted for. Reuters independently reported continuing rescue operations and efforts to reach damaged hydropower infrastructure.

Why it matters

The disaster is a major Asian humanitarian emergency and highlights the vulnerability of mountain communities and critical infrastructure.

South Korea · East Asia

South Korea sentences Unification Church leader to two years in prison

Reuters and AP reported that the Seoul Central District Court sentenced Han Hak-ja to two years in prison over bribery, political funding and related corruption offenses. The church has indicated it plans to appeal.

Why it matters

The ruling is part of South Korea’s broader legal reckoning over political influence and corruption linked to the former presidential circle.
