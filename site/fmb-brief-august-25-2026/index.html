Philippines · Defence + public policy

The Philippines wants more military drones. The harder question is what capability it is actually building.

Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. said on August 25 that the Philippines plans to expand its unmanned capabilities through both development and acquisition. Reuters reported that the effort includes drones and other unmanned systems as Manila continues modernizing its armed forces amid persistent maritime tensions.

The policy direction matters because unmanned systems can widen surveillance, maritime-domain awareness, logistics and strike options without requiring the same manpower or cost profile as larger crewed platforms.

FMB read

The useful test is not how many drones the Philippines buys. It is whether sensors, communications, training, maintenance, doctrine and local industrial capability develop with them. Hardware without integration creates inventory. Capability requires a system.

Philippines · Politics + accountability

Day 18 of the Duterte trial moves deeper into the confidential-funds chain.

Vice President Sara Duterte’s impeachment trial enters its 18th day on August 25. INQUIRER.net reports that Office of the Vice President Assistant Chief of Staff Lemuel Ortonio is set to testify about the alleged handling of ₱500 million in confidential funds. Prosecutors are seeking to have him declared a hostile witness.

Duterte denies wrongdoing. The Senate impeachment court has indicated that it hopes to complete proceedings before the end of the year, while House prosecutors are targeting the end of September to finish presenting their evidence.

Why it matters

The trial is now increasingly about records, money movement and witness credibility rather than opening narratives. Coverage should keep four things separate: the prosecution’s allegations, what witnesses actually establish, what the defence contests and what the impeachment court ultimately finds.

Philippines · Economy + markets

The BSP is two days from a decision where the explanation may matter more than the rate move.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas is scheduled to hold its fourth monetary-policy meeting of 2026 on August 27. The decision arrives after second-quarter GDP growth slowed to 2.3% year on year while July headline inflation remained at 6.2%, above the government’s 3% target.

That combination gives policymakers no easy direction. Tighter policy can help contain inflation and currency pressure but can also weigh on already-soft investment and demand. Easier policy can support activity but risks loosening conditions while imported energy remains volatile.

What matters Thursday

Watch the BSP’s risk hierarchy. If it stresses weak growth, markets will hear room for support. If it emphasizes inflation, energy and the peso, the message will be that price stability still dominates. The language can move expectations even if the headline rate surprises no one.

Technology · AI + startups

Echelon Philippines opens with a better AI question: ROI or RIP?

Echelon Philippines 2026 opens today at SMX Aura in Taguig. Its official agenda is unusually explicit about moving past AI theatre. A Day 1 panel titled “ROI or RIP: How to Measure Whether Your AI Spend Is Working” brings founders and operators together to discuss actual costs, savings and implementation outcomes.

The programme also includes government leaders from DOST, DICT and DTI, startup demonstrations, discussions on enterprise pilots, AI lending and access to capital, and debates over who actually gets funded in the Philippine startup ecosystem.

FMB read

This is the right correction for Philippine AI strategy. “Uses AI” is no longer differentiation. Better questions are: who pays, what expensive problem disappears, what gets measurably faster or cheaper, and what advantage remains when competitors can access similar models?

Source:Echelon Philippines 2026 — official agenda
Middle East · Energy + global markets

Markets are treating sanctions as less dangerous than missiles — for now.

Brent crude settled at $88.58 per barrel on August 25, down 3.9%, while U.S. crude settled at $82.36. Reuters reported that traders largely shrugged off Washington’s expanded pressure on Iran, viewing economic sanctions as less immediately threatening to physical oil supply than another military escalation.

That relief should not be confused with normalization. Reuters separately estimates that almost half of global oil production now comes from countries affected by conflict in 2026, while the Strait of Hormuz remains a central geopolitical risk.

Philippine implication

Lower oil is helpful for an energy-importing economy, but one day’s price move does not remove the structural exposure. Fuel, freight, aviation, the peso and inflation all remain sensitive to whether Hormuz shipping actually normalizes.

Media · Conflict + accountability

One year after Nasser Hospital, Reuters and AP are still asking for a full explanation.

On August 25, Reuters Editor-in-Chief Alessandra Galloni and Associated Press Executive Editor Julie Pace marked one year since Israeli strikes on Nasser Hospital in Gaza killed five journalists, including Reuters cameraman Hussam al-Masri and AP-affiliated visual journalist Mariam Dagga.

The editors said the explanations received so far remain insufficient and again called on Israel to provide a full account of the incident and to protect journalists working in conflict zones.

Why it matters

Journalist safety is not a niche media concern. When independent observers cannot work safely, the public becomes more dependent on military statements, partisan sources and unverifiable social media. Accountability for attacks on journalists is therefore part of accountability for the information environment itself.

Source:Reuters and AP joint statement
Climate · Water + disaster risk

El Niño and Habagat can be true at the same time — and that is exactly why climate planning is difficult.

PAGASA continues to monitor El Niño conditions that are expected to strengthen and persist into the first half of 2027. At the same time, the southwest monsoon remains capable of producing heavy rain over western Luzon, including Zambales.

That combination matters because climate risk is not a single switch between “wet” and “dry.” A country can face drought and agricultural stress in some areas while other regions experience monsoon flooding, rough seas and landslide risk.

FMB read

The policy response has to be equally two-track: water conservation, crop planning and energy preparation for El Niño, while maintaining drainage, flood warnings, evacuation systems and coastal advisories for monsoon rainfall.
