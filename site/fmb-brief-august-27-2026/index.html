Philippines · Economy + markets

The BSP tightened again. The peso still fell to a record low.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas raised its key policy rate by another 25 basis points on August 27, bringing the target reverse repurchase rate to 5.0%. The overnight deposit and lending rates were raised to 4.5% and 5.5% respectively. It was the BSP’s third consecutive rate increase this year.

The central bank cited persistent inflation risk even as headline inflation eased to 6.2% in July. Core inflation remained at 4.2%, while policymakers pointed to volatile oil, a potentially severe El Niño and wage adjustments as additional risks.

The peso nevertheless closed at a new record low of about ₱61.89 per U.S. dollar. The PSEi also fell 2.16% to 6,004.58 as investors weighed the effect of higher borrowing costs on an economy that grew only 2.3% year on year in the second quarter.

FMB read

The rate hike is not a victory over inflation; it is an admission that inflation risk is still serious enough to justify slowing demand further. The peso’s record low adds another layer: even tighter policy is not automatically enough to overpower global dollar strength, energy risk and domestic growth concerns. For households and businesses, the combination means expensive credit and expensive imports can coexist.

Philippines · Digital policy + media

Meta and Roblox have agreed to stronger child-safety controls for Philippine users.

Information and Communications Technology Secretary Henry Aguda said Meta and Roblox agreed during meetings with Philippine officials in Singapore to strengthen protections for young users. The commitments include age-verification systems, improved parental controls, faster removal of harmful content and closer cooperation with law enforcement.

The agreement comes while Philippine lawmakers are considering stronger online-safety rules for minors and after the livestreamed Zamboanga school shooting intensified debate over violent content, platform responsibility and youth access.

Roblox plans to open a Philippine office by October, while Meta is expected to send a team to establish a timeline for expanding its local presence, according to Reuters.

Why it matters

This moves child safety from a voluntary brand promise toward an operational expectation. Age assurance, default controls, rapid takedowns and law-enforcement cooperation can now become benchmarks against which platforms are judged. For media and technology companies, safety is increasingly part of product quality and reputation, not a back-office compliance issue.

Source:Reuters — Meta and Roblox commitments
Philippines · Politics + accountability

The impeachment court is trying to keep the trial inside the courtroom.

The Senate impeachment court has tightened restrictions on public commentary as Vice President Sara Duterte’s trial continues. Presiding Officer Francis Escudero has reminded parties to avoid public discussion of the merits while the court weighs witness testimony and procedural disputes.

Office of the Vice President Assistant Chief of Staff Lemuel Ortonio has continued testifying about confidential-fund processes. Separately, Palace officials rejected Duterte’s claim that the administration was prioritizing impeachment and political survival over flooding and poverty.

Duterte denies wrongdoing and has argued that prosecutors must prove the allegations through evidence rather than political rhetoric.

FMB read

The trial’s legitimacy depends on two systems working at once: evidence has to be tested rigorously, and political actors have to avoid contaminating the proceeding with a parallel campaign outside the chamber. The stricter decorum rules are therefore not cosmetic. They are part of protecting the credibility of whatever judgment eventually emerges.

Middle East · Energy + geopolitics

Hormuz is negotiating a way forward without being normal again.

Iran’s Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran is formulating conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including an end to the regional war and changes to U.S. sanctions and port restrictions. Iran and Oman have discussed a shipping corridor through their waters, but important details remain unsettled.

Shipping traffic through the strait rose only slightly on August 27 and remained far below normal pre-war levels. Oil prices reversed recent losses after U.S. President Donald Trump rejected a return to earlier ceasefire terms; Brent settled up 2.1% at about $89.70 a barrel.

Philippine implication

The risk premium can fall before the physical corridor is repaired. For the Philippines, the important measures are not only daily oil prices but vessel traffic, shipping insurance and sustained export volumes. Until those normalize, fuel and inflation remain exposed to another geopolitical shock.

Technology · AI + physical systems

Anthropic is pushing AI agents beyond software and into machines.

Anthropic introduced a research preview of its Model Hardware Standard, a framework designed to let AI agents operate programmable physical devices such as microscopes and robotic arms. The company says potential uses range from drug-discovery experiments to laser calibration on quantum computers.

The standard is intended to allow devices and AI agents to communicate across networks and coordinate autonomous workflows. Anthropic is sharing an early version with partners for safety evaluations before making it open source.

The announcement lands a day after Nvidia’s latest results reinforced the scale of AI infrastructure demand, with data-center revenue reaching $89 billion and the company forecasting $108 billion in quarterly revenue.

FMB read

This is a more consequential direction than another chatbot feature. When AI can safely control instruments, machinery and laboratory systems, the economic value moves into manufacturing, research and operations. The risk also changes: reliability, permissions, physical safety and auditability become as important as model accuracy.

Sources:Reuters — Anthropic Model Hardware StandardReuters — Nvidia earnings
Ukraine · Conflict + infrastructure

Russia is hitting the systems that keep Ukraine’s economy moving.

Russia launched a large overnight and daytime barrage across Ukraine on August 27 using ballistic missiles and 258 drones, according to Ukrainian officials cited by Reuters. Targets included ports, energy facilities, industrial sites, distribution centers and other logistics infrastructure.

Ukraine said newly delivered Patriot interceptors helped its forces shoot down seven ballistic missiles, but strikes still damaged commercial facilities and disrupted rail travel. One person was reported killed and two injured in Kharkiv, while warehouse and distribution facilities around Kyiv were destroyed.

Russia also warned it could strike British military targets inside or outside Ukraine in response to Ukrainian attacks using British-supplied long-range missiles.

Why it matters

The war is increasingly a contest over economic endurance. Ports, warehouses, railways, energy systems and distribution networks determine whether a country can supply civilians, move exports and sustain military operations. Infrastructure damage therefore matters even when front lines barely move.

Climate · Weather + Zambales

An LPA inside PAR may strengthen while Habagat is already affecting Zambales.

PAGASA’s 4 a.m. August 27 forecast placed a low pressure area about 1,030 km east of Northern Luzon. The system was inside the Philippine Area of Responsibility and was being monitored for possible development into a tropical depression. If it becomes a named cyclone inside PAR, it would be called Pilandok.

The southwest monsoon is already affecting Luzon and the western Visayas. PAGASA forecast occasional rains over Zambales and Occidental Mindoro, with possible flash floods or landslides during moderate to heavy rain. Moderate to rough coastal waters of 1.5 to 3.4 meters were forecast over Luzon and the western Visayas.

Rainfall advisories during the day also covered parts of Zambales, reinforcing that local risk exists before the LPA becomes a named storm.

Local implication

Masinloc and the rest of Zambales do not need a named typhoon for weather to become operationally serious. Habagat can already disrupt roads, classes, fishing, tourism and low-lying communities. Local digital platforms earn trust by keeping advisories current, timestamped and useful before conditions become dramatic.

The signal in one sentence

The institutions that matter today are being tested not by one crisis, but by overlapping constraints: inflation, currency pressure, online harm, energy chokepoints, physical infrastructure and weather.

  4. AI moving from screens into physical operations.

What FMB should watch

Watch whether today’s policy moves change the underlying system.

The BSP after the hike. Watch the peso, lending conditions and the next inflation expectations. A higher policy rate only works if it anchors expectations without deepening the slowdown more than necessary.

Meta and Roblox implementation. The agreement matters only if age verification, parental controls and harmful-content takedowns become real product changes in the Philippines.

Hormuz vessel traffic. Diplomatic language will move markets, but physical shipping data will show whether the energy corridor is actually normalizing.

Anthropic’s hardware standard. Watch safety testing and early industrial partners. Physical autonomy raises a higher bar than software-only agents.

Ukraine’s logistics systems. Repeated strikes on ports, warehouses and rail infrastructure can have consequences for exports, food markets and economic resilience even without dramatic territorial shifts.

PAGASA’s next LPA update. The immediate local issue for Zambales is Habagat rainfall; the next strategic question is whether the LPA intensifies and further enhances monsoon conditions.

FMB bottom line

The risk is no longer theoretical.

Inflation risk became another rate hike.

Currency risk became a record-low peso.

Online-safety pressure became concrete platform commitments.

Energy risk became a negotiation over access to one of the world’s most important shipping corridors.

AI ambition is becoming physical machinery and laboratory control.

War is increasingly striking warehouses, ports and distribution systems.

And weather risk is already affecting Zambales before the LPA has a name.

The useful question now is not whether institutions recognize the pressure.

It is whether their responses are strong enough to change the outcome.
